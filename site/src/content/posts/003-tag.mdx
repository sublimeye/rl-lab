**Short answer:**

- **It learned to run laps.** Once it gets clear, it circles the arena at full speed, two to
  three laps in 17 seconds, and the test bots never catch it after that. Every catch happens in
  the first few seconds.
- **Who it trains against matters.** Trained against one simple chaser, it escapes that kind of
  chaser 98% of the time, but escapes a chaser that aims ahead of it only 54% of the time.
  Trained against a varied mix of chasers: 94% and 78%.
- **The reward can be simple.** Points for staying alive, a big penalty for being caught. A
  bonus for keeping distance didn't make it better.

## The setup

The arena is 12 by 8 units with two round pillars. Both squares have the same momentum
physics as the cube in [the first post](../001-cube-to-flag/): pick a direction, accelerate,
slow down by drag. The blue square tops out at 4 units per second; the chaser at a fraction of
that.

The network sees nine numbers, 20 times a second: its own position and velocity, where the
chaser is relative to it, the chaser's velocity, and the distance between them. It picks one of
nine actions: stand still, or accelerate in one of eight directions. It doesn't see the pillars;
it has to learn where they are from its own position, like the walls in the first post.

## Rewards

Every step it isn't caught, it gets **+0.01**. Being caught costs **−5** and ends the
episode. Surviving 20 seconds (400 steps) earns +4 at most.

The reasoning:

- **Why not just "+1 if you survive"?** Early on, nearly every episode ends in a catch within a
  couple of seconds, so a reward only at the end almost never arrives. A reward every step
  says "longer is better" from the very first episode.
- **Why a big penalty?** With only the per-step reward, getting caught one second before the end
  costs a hundredth of a point. The penalty makes any catch clearly worse than running out the
  clock.
- **Distance bonus:** I also added up to +0.01 per step for keeping distance (the full bonus at 6
  units or more), expecting it to help. A run without it did at least as well: 85% escapes
  against the chaser that aims ahead, compared with 74–82% for the three seeds with the bonus.
  That's one seed, so I won't claim the bonus hurts, but it wasn't needed.

## Who it trains against

The chaser during training is a script, not a person. I wrote two kinds:

- **Pursuit bot:** runs straight at where the blue square is now, going around pillars.
- **Intercept bot:** runs at where the blue square will be, based on its current velocity.

I trained two versions, three seeds each, 5 million steps:

- **One simple chaser:** always the pursuit bot at 85% speed, reacting instantly.
- **Mixed chasers:** every episode draws a new chaser. Pursuit or intercept, 75–100% speed,
  0.05–0.25 seconds reaction time, half of them limited to eight directions like a keyboard,
  and how far ahead the intercept bot aims varies too.

Then I tested every saved version against fixed chasers: 200 start positions each, the same
starts for every model. One test chaser is meant to behave a bit like a person on a keyboard:
it aims ahead, moves in eight directions and only changes direction every 0.2 seconds.

The simple version is a specialist: nearly perfect against the bot it knows, close to a coin flip
against one that thinks a step ahead. The mixed version gives up 3–4 points against the pursuit
bot and gains 20–27 points against everything else.

For scale: standing still gets caught in about 2 seconds, and running straight away from the
chaser in about 3. Both lose all 200 episodes against every test chaser, because running
straight away ends against a wall.

## What it learned

After the first few seconds, the final versions I measured (five of the seven) all do the same
thing: run laps around the arena at close to full speed, two to three laps in the 17 seconds
after the opening. Against the pursuit bot they hug the walls (the mixed versions spend 70–79% of
that time within half a unit of the edge), and the bot just follows. Against the intercept bot,
the mixed versions run a tighter loop away from the walls (6–37% of the time near the edge),
staying about 1.8 units ahead of it.

In the example above, the simple version heads for the top-left corner and then down the left
wall, the way it would against the pursuit bot. The intercept bot doesn't follow; it cuts
across the middle and meets it in the bottom-left corner.

It mostly ignores the pillars. I expected it to learn to circle one and keep it between itself
and the chaser. The versions I checked spend 5% of their time near a pillar or less. Laps of the
whole arena are simpler and work against these bots.

Every catch happens early. Across the three final mixed versions and all seven test chasers,
87% of catches came in the first 3 seconds, and the latest was at 6.25 seconds. That also
explains why chaser speed barely matters in the table: in the first couple of seconds, a slower
chaser hasn't fallen behind yet. When the chaser starts more than 6 units away, the seed 1 mixed
version escapes the intercept bot 89% of the time; from under 5 units, 71%. (The game always
starts you at least 5 units apart.)

## Versions you can play

The **Blue square** menu in the game switches between these, all seed 1, all scored against bots
at 90% speed:

| Version | vs pursuit bot | vs intercept bot | vs keyboard-like bot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Untrained | 0% | 0% | 0% |
| 250k steps, mixed chasers | 73% | 45% | 49% |
| 5M steps, mixed chasers | 94% | 83% | 85% |
| 5M steps, one simple chaser | 98% | 57% | 64% |

The untrained network moves more or less at random and gets caught in about 2.5 seconds. Against
the final versions, try cutting across the middle of its lap instead of following it.

## What I take from it

- **The opponent is part of the environment.** Training against one script taught it to beat
  that script. Varying the opponent did here what varying the start position did in
  [post 2](../002-does-more-training-help/).
- **Simple rewards were enough.** Alive +0.01, caught −5. The distance bonus I added to help
  made no measurable difference.
- **It found a strategy I didn't expect.** I thought it would use the pillars. It runs laps
  instead, because that's enough against these chasers.
- **The obvious next step is to train the chaser too** (self-play), so each side keeps finding the
  other's weak spots, instead of me writing better scripts.

## Cost

All seven 5M-step runs (two setups × three seeds, plus the one without the distance bonus)
trained in parallel in 8.4 minutes on an M1 Pro, CPU only. Evaluating every checkpoint against
the seven test chasers took under two minutes.

## Run it yourself

```sh
for s in 1 2 3; do
  uv run experiments/003-tag/train.py --chasers simple --steps 5000000 --seed $s --name simple-s$s &
  uv run experiments/003-tag/train.py --chasers mixed --steps 5000000 --seed $s --name mixed-s$s &
done
uv run experiments/003-tag/train.py --chasers mixed --rewards nodistance --steps 5000000 \
  --seed 1 --name nodist-s1 &
wait
uv run experiments/003-tag/evaluate.py simple-s1 simple-s2 simple-s3 mixed-s1 mixed-s2 mixed-s3 nodist-s1
uv run python -c "import sys; sys.path.insert(0, 'experiments/003-tag'); import evaluate; evaluate.export_report(0)"
uv run experiments/003-tag/summarize.py
cd site && npm test   # the browser simulation must match Python
```
