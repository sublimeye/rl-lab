In [the first post](../001-cube-to-flag/), a cube learned to cross a lava arena in a million
training steps. Here I trained the same setup ten times longer, on three random seeds, and
checked every saved version properly: 200 episodes each, instead of the four replays I eyeballed
last time.

**Short answer:** training longer polished what the cube already knew. It didn't teach it anything new.

- **Capable early:** every seed first reached the flag in 99% of episodes by 375,000 steps.
- **Not stable until much later:** after that, the seeds kept slipping back (one dropped to 0%).
  From 5 million steps on, all three stayed at 100%.
- **Faster:** 7.1 seconds to the flag at 300k steps, 5.2 s at 1M, 4.7 s at 10M. Most of that
  came by 1.5M.
- **Not more general:** from start positions it never trained on, success went from 71% at 1M
  to 76% at 10M, and was flat after 5M.
- **What fixed that:** starting each training episode in a random spot. After 3M steps (4
  minutes), it reached the flag from 99% of those starts.

- **Knows nothing (0 steps):** the network is random. The cube jitters around and drifts until
  it hits the lava band or the clock runs out. All four of these runs end in lava; across 200
  test episodes it's 60% lava, 40% out of time.
- **Halfway (150k):** it has found the gap on the right and picks up most of the coins
  above the lava band. Then it doesn't know where to go: two runs wander in the top corridor until
  time runs out, one dips back into the band, one clips the lava pool.
- **Final (10M):** a smooth curve through the gap, three coins on the way, at the flag in under
  five seconds.

The literal halfway point of the 10M run, 5M steps, scores the same as the final version on
every measure below, which is the first hint that the second 5 million steps didn't buy much.

## How I measured it

Every saved checkpoint plays 200 episodes in four conditions:

| | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Usual starts** | Near the bottom-left corner, like in training |
| **Anywhere starts** | Anywhere below the lava band; it never started there in training |
| **Best action** | Always do the action the network rates highest (how you'd deploy it) |
| **Sampled actions** | Roll the dice using the network's probabilities (how it behaves while training) |

I call a checkpoint **capable** when at least 99% of episodes reach the flag.

All three seeds first became capable between 300,000 and 375,000 steps. But that wasn't the end
of it. Seed 1 hit 100% at 300k, then at 375k *every* best-action episode timed out, then it was
back to 100% by 500k. Seed 1 dropped again to 46% at 3M steps.

Counting every check after a seed was first capable: 8 of 24 checks before 1M steps fell
below 99%, 3 of 12 between 1M and 5M, and none of the 9 after 5M. So the extra training did buy
something: stability. It took 5 million steps to get there, for two of the three seeds. The
third was stable from 475,000.

Once the task is learned, sampled actions are steadier than best actions. My guess at why: with
best actions, a small change in the network can flip the top choice in some spot for every
episode at once, while sampling blurs that over many episodes.

Three-seed means, comparing the version from the first post (1M steps) with ten times the
training:

| | 1M steps | 10M steps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Usual starts, best action | 93% (worst seed 79%) | 100% (every seed) |
| Usual starts, sampled actions | 99.8% | 99.5% |
| Seconds to the flag | 5.2 | 4.7 |
| Coins collected, out of 5 | 2.5 | 2.9 |
| Anywhere starts, best action | 71% | 76% |

Nine million extra steps bought half a second on the route, reliable behaviour with the best
action, and the three upper coins nearly every time. From the usual start it still skips the two
coins under the lava band. And it barely got better at starting somewhere new.

- a strip along the top-left, just under the lava band, where the cube times out;
- a column under the left edge of the gap, where at 1M it steers straight into the corner of

## Fix: start somewhere else

The cube only ever started in one corner, so it only learned the states along one route. It
doesn't see the map; it sees its position and speed. A position it has never been in is a
guess.

So I changed one thing: every training episode starts at a random point below the lava band,
the same area as the "anywhere" test. Same rewards, same network, same PPO settings. Three new
seeds, 3 million steps each.

From anywhere below the band it reached 96% by 1M steps and 99.3% at 3M (seeds: 100%, 98%,
100%). From the usual corner it's at 100% for all three. On the start map above, 491 of 492
start positions reach the flag. That's more than the 10M-step run managed, in under a third of
the steps.

It's slightly slower from the usual start (4.9–5.1 s instead of 4.7 s), because it often
detours for the lower coins now.

## What I take from it

- **Check the behaviour you care about, not the training reward.** In the first post, training
  said "almost always reaches the flag" at 1M steps. With best actions, seed 1 made it 79% of the
  time.
- **More steps polish; they don't generalize.** Ten times the training made the route faster
  and the policy steadier, and moved success from new starts by five points.
- **New skills came from new experience.** Varied starts got to 99% from anywhere in a third of
  the steps. With random starts it also learned to grab the lower coins (3.6 per episode instead
  of 2.9), because it now passes them.
- **Seeds matter.** Same code, same settings: one seed was stable at 475,000 steps, the other
  two at 5 million.
- **"Capable" needs a definition.** For two of the three seeds, "reached 99% once" happened at
  300,000 steps and "stays above 99%" at 5 million.

## Cost

The three 10M-step runs trained in parallel in 13.8 minutes on an M1 Pro (CPU only, two
threads each). The three random-start runs took 4.1 minutes. Evaluation plays 800 episodes for every
saved checkpoint (29 per 10M run), batched so the network handles 200 episodes at once.

## Run it yourself

```sh
# three 10M-step runs in parallel (~14 min on an M1 Pro)
for s in 1 2 3; do
  uv run experiments/001-cube-to-flag/train.py --steps 10000000 --seed $s \
    --name long-s$s --schedule dense --threads 2 &
done; wait
# random-start runs
for s in 1 2 3; do
  uv run experiments/001-cube-to-flag/train.py --variant randstart --steps 3000000 --seed $s \
    --name rand-s$s --schedule dense --threads 2 &
done; wait
uv run experiments/001-cube-to-flag/evaluate.py long-s1 long-s2 long-s3 rand-s1 rand-s2 rand-s3
uv run experiments/001-cube-to-flag/summarize.py
```
