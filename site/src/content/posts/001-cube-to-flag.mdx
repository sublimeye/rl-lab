## The arena

A 10 × 10 room. The cube starts at the bottom left, the flag is at the top left, and a lava
band across the middle means the only way up is through the gap on the right. A second lava pool
at the top leaves a corridor back to the flag. There's one wall near the start, and five coins.

The cube has momentum. Each step (1/20 of a simulated second) it picks one of five actions: push
up, down, left, right, or do nothing. It sees its own position and velocity, plus which coins are
still there. It does not see the map; it has to learn it.

| Event | Reward |
| --- | --- |
| Every step | −0.005 |
| Coin | +1 |
| Reach the flag | +10, episode ends |
| Touch lava | −3, episode ends |
| 500 steps without either | episode ends |

The policy is a small neural network (two layers of 64) trained with PPO from
[Stable-Baselines3](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/), eight copies of the arena running
in parallel.

## First try: it learned to hide

With only the rewards in the table, the cube never got anywhere. Early random runs found lava
more often than coins, so the policy learned that the lava is the thing to avoid. By 20,000 steps
it drove straight into the bottom edge of the arena, as far from the lava as it could get, and
waited out the clock there: −2.5 per episode, every episode, for the rest of the million steps.

This is the classic sparse-reward problem. The flag is far away and the agent has no hint that
it exists until it stumbles into it, which a cautious policy never does.

## Fix: reward getting closer

The second version adds one more reward: each step, the cube earns 0.5 × the amount it shortened
its *walking* distance to the flag, and loses the same if it moves away. Walking distance goes
around walls and lava rather than through them, so the hint points through the gap on the right
instead of straight into the lava band. It's precomputed once for a grid over the arena with
Dijkstra.

The bonus only depends on how far the cube is from the flag, so a round trip earns nothing
overall: the cube can't farm it by going in circles. (This is a simple form of
[potential-based shaping](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pabbeel/cs287-fa09/readings/NgHaradaRussell-shaping-ICML1999.pdf).)

## What the checkpoints show

- **20k steps:** it wanders, and sometimes picks up a coin by accident.
- **50k:** it has found the gap on the right, but all four runs clip the corner of the lava band
  on the way through.
- **100k:** it makes it through the gap and collects the three upper coins, then dips back
  into the band while heading left.
- **200k:** it reaches the top-left corridor and parks there, a couple of units short of the
  flag, until time runs out.
- **350k and later:** all four runs reach the flag. The route gets faster with more training:
  about 6.6 seconds at 350k, 5 seconds at one million.

It never takes the two coins below the lava band. Picking them up would mean a detour along the
bottom first, and PPO settled on the shorter route. That's a local optimum, not necessarily the
best possible score.

## Cost

Both runs took under 80 seconds for one million steps on an M1 Pro, on the CPU. For a network
this small, the CPU is faster than the Apple GPU. The replay data for this page is about 280 KB of
JSON.

## Run it yourself

```sh
git clone https://github.com/sublimeye/rl-lab && cd rl-lab
uv sync
uv run experiments/001-cube-to-flag/train.py --variant shaped --steps 1000000
uv run experiments/001-cube-to-flag/export.py --variant shaped
cd site && npm install && npm run dev
```

The arena, physics and rewards are all in
[`cube_env.py`](https://github.com/sublimeye/rl-lab/blob/main/experiments/001-cube-to-flag/cube_env.py).
