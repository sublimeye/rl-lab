[Post 16](../016-flawless/) left a planner that lands every course and a network that, copying
it, lands 1 to 6 of 200 alone. Posts 14, 15 and 16 each went straight to training. This one
looks first:
1. **Why does the copy fail?** Answered from post 16's saved flights, training nothing.
2. **What does that say to change, and does it work?**

The courses, the 45° drone and the 59 moves are post 16's.

**New here? The words in this post, in plain terms:**
- **Course (the gauntlet).** A simulated flight of 60–120 m over water to a landing pad, past
  birds and missiles that chase the drone and "frogs" that leap at it.
- **Planner.** A pilot that thinks ahead by trial: every 0.2 s it imagines about a thousand
  ways the next few seconds could go and follows the best. It lands every course, slowly.
- **Network.** A fast formula with many tuned numbers, learned from examples.
- **Move.** What the network decides, 20 times a second: one of 59, such as "cruise at 7 m up
  and 10 m/s", a sharp dodge, or a wait beside the pad. A hand-written controller turns the
  move into motor commands.
- **Copying.** Training the network to give the planner's answer.
- **Safe and fatal moves.** After a safe move the planner still finds a way through. After a
  fatal one, every plan it tries ends in a crash.
- **Practice.** The network flies by itself, thousands of courses at once, and is nudged
  toward what ended in a landing and away from what ended in a crash (reinforcement learning).
- **Test, fresh and dev courses.** Courses nothing trains on: 200 test (post 14's), 200 fresh
  (post 16's), and 1,000 dev courses for comparing versions, so the first two are flown once,
  at the end.

**Short answer:**
- **The copy had memorised its flights.** It picks the planner's move 98% of the time on
  flights it trained on and 63% on flights it hasn't seen, where about one decision in eight
  is fatal.
- **It was no better than not looking.** A pilot that ignores every threat lands 4 of 200.
  Post 16's network lands 3, and none of 200 courses with no threats at all.
- **The planner's pick is partly a coin toss.** It usually has 37 safe moves to choose from.
  Nudge the world by the sensors' noise and it picks a different one 1 time in 10.
- **Same flights, a different question.** Fitted to "any move the planner found safe", the
  network lands 48 of 200 test courses, with no new data.
- **Then practice.** After 45 minutes on a laptop, learning only from how its own flights
  end: **162 of 200 test courses and 163 of 200 fresh ones**, with no planner at flight time.

## Why the copy failed

All of this comes from post 16's saved data: 1,900 flights, 410,000 moments, each with the
planner's move and its verdict on every other move.

**The accuracy was measured wrong.** Post 16's 90–94% held out single moments at random,
and each had its neighbours, 0.05 s away in the same flight, in the training set. With whole
flights held out:

| Post 16's recipe | Flights it trained on | Flights it hasn't seen |
|---|---|---|
| Picks the planner's move | 98% | 63% |
| Picks a fatal move | 0.9% | 13% |
| The same, with a bird or missile about to pass | 2% | 24% |

**Every part of the task fails.** Against a pilot that ignores every threat (cruise at 7 m,
then land), 200 test courses each:

| Course | Ignores every threat | Post 16's network |
|---|---|---|
| No threats at all | 198 | 0 |
| A bird at every waypoint | 25 | 0 |
| The missile at every waypoint | 5 | 0 |
| The two frogs only | 13 | 6 |
| The full gauntlet | 4 | 3 |

On the clear courses 193 of its 200 flights leave the course without landing. Every course
it trained on had a frog on the pad.

**The planner's pick is partly arbitrary.** Of the 47 or so moves it weighs, 37 are typically
safe, and it takes the best-scoring one after a tie-break. I had it decide 1,050 situations
twice, the second time nudged by the size of the sensors' noise (5 cm, 0.1 m/s). It picked a
different move in 10% of them, and in 3% a move the un-nudged planner found fatal. More
flights help slowly: eight times as many take the fatal picks from 16.8% to 14.1%.

**What this points to.** The planner's single pick is the wrong thing to copy: the end of a
4.5 s search plus a tie-break, too fine for 1,900 flights to pin down. What a pilot needs is
which moves get it killed. I wrote that down, with a line at which to stop each attempt,
before training anything.

## The same flights, a different question

The planner's verdict on *every* move was already in the saved data. So instead of "put your
probability on the planner's pick", the network is fitted to "put it on moves the planner
found safe, any of them":

| Fitted to | Fatal picks, unseen flights | Dev courses landed (of 200) |
|---|---|---|
| The planner's pick (post 16's recipe) | 13.2% | 12 |
| The planner's pick, regularised, no last-move input | 11.8% | 13 |
| Any safe move | 11.6% | 10 |
| Any safe move, regularised, no last-move input | **7.5%** | **42** |

Neither change does much alone. Together they nearly halve the fatal picks. ("Regularised":
the usual guards against memorising.)

I also took away the planner's wind estimate, which replays every update on a copy of the
simulator. That cost nothing (40 of 200). Drawing its moves from its own probabilities, this
network lands **48 of 200 test courses and 43 of 200 fresh ones**.

## Practice

The planner's verdicts say which moves are safe where the planner flew. The network's own
flights say it wherever the network goes. So it practises (the method is PPO):
- the only teacher is how each flight ends: post 14's reward (progress toward the pad, +20
  for landing, −10 for any failure, a small cost for time);
- it starts from the safe-move network;
- 8,192 drones fly at once on the laptop.

The line set beforehand: stop unless it lands 5 points more of the dev courses within 45
minutes. The first run was 16 points up after 3. I ran it three ways:

| Practice | Dev courses landed (of 1,000) |
|---|---|
| From nothing, 15 minutes | 0 |
| From the copy, still fitting the saved flights as a side task, 45 minutes | 749 |
| From the copy, no side task, 45 minutes | **843** |

- **The copy is what makes practice work in this time.** From nothing, the same practice
  landed 0 or 1 of 400 dev courses at every check.
- **Holding on to the saved flights cost 9 points.** I added the side task so practice
  couldn't forget what the planner had shown. It didn't need it.

| Pilot | Test courses (of 200) | Fresh courses (of 200) |
|---|---|---|
| Post 16's network (copies the planner's pick) | 3 | 5 |

move lands a few fewer: 156 and 157.

## What it still can't do

One kind of encounter at every waypoint, 200 test courses each:

| Encounter | Ignores every threat | After practice |
|---|---|---|
| A bird | 175 of 407 hit | 0 of 665 |
| Two birds | 183 of 394 | 9 of 661 |
| The missile | 195 of 270 | 13 of 652 |
| A bird + the missile | 199 of 261 | 27 of 641 |
| A crossing flock | 122 of 492 | 32 of 623 |

- **Flocks are its worst encounter**: 1 in 19 hits it.
- **On the test courses** its 38 failed flights are 15 caught by frogs, 11 hit by the
  missile, 8 by birds, and 4 crashed or off the course.
- **It can't land on an unguarded pad.** With no frog on the pad it lands none of 200: 139
  fly on past the pad and off the far end, 61 climb out through the ceiling. Every course it
  has seen had that frog. (The table counts its dodging on the way there.)
- **It doesn't work the sticks.** A hand-written controller turns each move into motor
  commands, from the drone's true state. Post 14's network worked the sticks itself, on a 30°
  drone, and landed 33: a different pilot, not a like-for-like match.

## What I take from it

- **Look before training.** Three posts of training gave 1 to 33. Under an hour with saved
  data showed the copy had memorised its flights, and what to ask instead.
- **Hold out whole flights.** 94% of held-out moves right and 3 of 200 courses landed were
  both true of the same network.
- **Copy what a search knows, not what it picks.** Which moves are safe carried over to new
  flights. Which of 37 safe moves won a tie-break did not.
- **The copy made practice possible; practice did most of the work.** And once practice
  starts, the teacher's examples are no longer worth holding on to.
- **Next:** unguarded pads and flocks in the practice courses, and a network that works the
  sticks itself, taught by this one. Unlike the planner, this teacher is quick to ask.

### How the copy fails, flight by flight

Without refitting anything: each round's network, on the next round's flights (which it never
trained on), picks the planner's move 59–67% of the time and a fatal one 11–13%, with no
improvement over five rounds, in both of post 16's runs. With a bird or missile about to pass
(closest approach within 2 s and 2 m) the fatal picks are 21–23%; with nothing in sight and no
frog near, 1–2%.

These are not rare slips. In a failed flight the first fatal move comes about 1 s before a
bird hit, 2 s before a missile hit and 4 s before a frog's, and it is one of three or four.
The flight is beyond saving (no safe move left) only in its last 0.6–0.8 s. Until then a safe
move existed and the network kept not taking it.

It also repeats itself. When the planner wants a different move from the one the network made
last, the network keeps its own 76–81% of the time. Hiding that input alone didn't help,
though (fatal picks 14.1%).

### Holding out flights, not moments

A flight is a few hundred moments, 0.05 s apart, and each looks almost like the one before. A
network that has seen a moment's neighbours can answer for it from memory. Post 16's fit ran
270 passes over its first 122,000 moments, to a training loss near zero, and its random
hold-out could not tell memory from skill. Here 197 of the 1,900 flights are set aside whole,
and every fit is also flown: 200 courses take a network a few seconds, so there is no reason to
trust a proxy.

### What "fatal" means here

At a decision the planner flies about 900 plans ahead, grouped by the move each starts with.
A move's score is its best plan's, under the worst of three guesses for when a frog strikes.
Under −500, every plan starting with that move crashes within the look-ahead. A plan holds its
first move for 0.6 s, so this is a little pessimistic about a move held for only 0.05 s; it is
the planner's judgement, not ground truth. Post 16 saved these scores for every move at every
decision and trained a second output on them, the "danger sense". Used as a veto on post 16's
network (skip any move it expects to fail), it lifts 3 landings to 17.

### Nudging the planner

Forty flights on courses no run had used. At every third decision a copy of the planner, with
the same memory and the same last plan, decides again from a nudged world: the drone's position
moved by 5 cm and its speed by 0.1 m/s, each bird and missile by 5 cm (one standard deviation,
the sizes of the sensor noise the network's inputs carry). The planner itself sees the true
state, so it never meets this noise; a network copying it always does.

### The safe-move fit

For a moment where at least one move is safe, the loss is minus the log of the probability the
network puts on the safe moves together, plus a tenth of the usual loss on the planner's own
pick. Where nothing is safe (a flight already lost), the usual loss. Against memorising:
dropout of 0.3, noise of 0.1 standard deviations on every input, weight decay of 0.01, and the
last-move inputs blanked. Same network as post 16's (512, 512 and 256 units), same 8,000
steps. Also tried on the same flights: the planner's scores as soft targets (10.5% fatal picks
on unseen flights, 26 of 200 dev courses); fewer steps (12.9%, 4 of 200); no last-move input
alone (14.1%, 7 of 200).

### What the pilot is, exactly

- **Inputs, 341:** post 14's 318 (the drone, the route, up to four pursuers with their closest
  approach, 3 s of each frog's motion), 16 for a memory of each frog's strikes (when the next
  two could start and end, worked out from the strikes seen within 40 m, as post 16's planner
  did), and 7 for the move made last.
- **Output:** a probability for each of the 59 moves. Landing runs and waits are only on offer
  within 25 m of the pad, as in the planner.
- **Controller:** post 15's, with post 16's gentler touchdown. It reads the drone's true
  position and speed and assumes still air and the average mass. Flown by a rule that only
  cruises and then lands, it lands 198 of 200 courses with no threats, so the assumption costs
  little.
- **Nothing else:** no search, no copies of the simulator, no planner object.

Drawing a move from the probabilities beats always taking the top one, before practice (48
against 28 test courses) and after (162 against 156). I haven't measured why.

### Practice, in numbers

PPO, 8 processes of 1,024 drones each, every course like a test course (60–120 m, every kind
of encounter, the pad's frog, a mid-route frog half the time). Each round is 48 decisions per
drone (2.4 s), then 3 passes over the 393,000 decisions in batches of 16,384. Learning rate
0.0001, clip 0.2, discount 0.995 per decision, an entropy bonus of 0.003. For the first 5
rounds only the value network learns. Where it was used, the side task (the safe-move loss on
the saved flights) had weight 0.3. About 60,000 decisions a second on an M1 Pro: 56,000 with
the side task, 63,000 without.

The two shorter comparisons were 15-minute runs. From nothing (a learning rate of 0.0003):
0, 0, 1, 0, 0, 0 of 400 dev courses drawn at its six checks, and 0 of 1,000 at the end; one
run, one setting. Without the side task: 260 of 400 after 49 million decisions, against 221
with it; the 45-minute run without it repeated those checks exactly.

### What I stopped, and what I didn't need

- **Carrying a run on.** Before the clean run without the side task, I let the first
  45-minute network practise on without it, with a new value network (I hadn't saved the old
  one). That set it back: 301 of 400 dev courses fell to 255, and it was at 284 when I stopped
  it after 13 minutes. The trainer now saves its value network.
- **More planner labels.** The plan had a second route: more of the planner's verdicts, on
  the new network's own flights, from a rented GPU. Practice passed its line first, so no GPU
  was rented for this post.
